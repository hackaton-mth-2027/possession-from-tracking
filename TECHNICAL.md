# Team possession from tracking: technical document

More than a Hack 2027, Initial Challenge. FC Barcelona (H) vs Athletic Club (A).
Submission: `outputs/submissions/dfl_bag_st_dflcd.csv`, regenerated by
`python scripts/make_submission.py`.

## 1. Approach in one paragraph

We learn "which team has the ball, or is the ball dead" from **seven public Bundesliga matches**
(DFL / Sportec open data, CC-BY 4.0), whose tracking carries a per-frame possession flag and a
ball-alive flag. Their clean tracking is degraded to look like this match's automatic tracking
(airborne balls drawn where the camera's line of sight meets the ground, jittered positions,
players dropping out, the ball missing as it does here in the same game state). Two families of
models on our tracking features give per-frame probabilities of which team has the ball:
gradient boosting, with physical signals (who can reach the ball's path first, who moves with
the ball), and temporal convolutional networks that look ±5 s around each frame. The dead-ball
models also learn from **64 World Cup 2022 matches** (PFF FC broadcast tracking), whose
dead ball we label from the events (ball out until the restart), as the provider does. A detector
of possession changes, also learned on the DFL matches, lowers the switching cost of a Viterbi
decoder where a change is likely. **Nothing in the pipeline is fitted on this match's
labels.** Model and decoder choices were made on a validation set built from Metrica's
three open matches (1,132 possessions in the provider's own convention). One choice is about the
labelling convention and not the model: which matches teach the dead ball. There we followed the
public ground truth, which CHALLENGE.md names as the reference for the convention. Otherwise the
5 public minutes serve as a check.

## 2. Pipeline

1. **Features** (`possession/features.py`), per frame, from the provided tactical tracking only:
   ball (seen, gap lengths, speed, position relative to lines and goals), each team's distance to
   the ball and players nearby, nearest-player continuity, "who moves towards the ball", team
   shapes (advance, length, width, speeds, walking share), rolling means over +-0.5 s and +-2 s,
   oriented by each team's attacking side (read at each kickoff). Plus physical signals
   (`possession/features_extra.py`):
   - interception: where the ball will be over the next 2 s (rolling friction), and which team's
     player gets there first, from each player's velocity, a 0.3 s reaction time and 7 m/s;
   - carrier: a player close to the ball and moving with it over ±0.25-0.5 s (on Metrica its
     sign agrees with the team in possession on 82% of live frames, against 75% for "nearest
     player");
   - goalkeeper: each team's deepest player with the ball close and slow.
2. **External training data**, converted to our coordinates and made noisy (`dfl.add_noise`):
   - DFL (`possession/dfl.py`, `tools/download_dfl.sh`): 7 matches, ~670 minutes, per-frame
     possession and ball-status flags;
   - PFF FC World Cup 2022 (`possession/pff.py`, `tools/download_pff.sh`): 64 matches, 108 hours,
     broadcast tracking resampled to 25 Hz. Labels from the events: the team of the last
     controlled on-ball action; dead ball from a ball out, or the end of the last action before a
     set piece, until the restart. PFF's raw ball already goes missing by game state as ours does
     (5% of live frames, 66% of dead-ball frames; ours 11% / 72%), so no gaps are added.
3. **Frame models**, 3 classes, each model on its own noise draw:
   - team, boosting (`scripts/team_features.py`): 9 gradient-boosting models on DFL with the
     physical signals, 6 with the ball missing at random places and 3 with it missing as in our
     match per game state (72% of dead-ball frames, 11% of live ones; the game state comes from a
     first model decoded with fixed costs, no labels);
   - team, temporal (`scripts/dfl_tcn.py`): 16 networks (7 dilated residual blocks over ±5 s,
     64 channels; 3 seeds and 5 variants: ±10 s, 12 epochs, state-noise copies, 128 channels,
     physical signals), 8 on DFL and the same 8 on DFL + Metrica's 3 open matches labelled by the
     challenge's controlled-possession rule (section 6);
   - dead ball (`scripts/pff_train.py`): 3 boosting models on DFL (state noise) + PFF.

   Team = home share averaged within each family, the two families half each; dead ball =
   average of the 3 DFL + PFF models.
4. **Change detector** (`scripts/dfl_change.py`): gradient boosting on direction-free change
   signals and the frame features. Trained on the DFL changes: frames within ±6 of a change
   against calm frames. On the official window's 12 changes it separates changes from calm frames
   with AUC 0.84, without ever being trained on them.
5. **Dead-ball boundary detector** (`scripts/dead_change.py`): the same kind of model for the ball
   going dead or play restarting, trained on the PFF matches (frames within ±6 of a live / dead
   transition against calm frames), with how much the players' speed, the walking share and the
   ball's visibility and speed change around each frame.
6. **Decoding** (`possession/decode.py`, `scripts/make_submission.py`): Viterbi per half over
   {H, A, D}, frame by frame. Team-switch cost max(4, 28 - 23 p_change); cost of entering or
   leaving dead ball max(4, 24 - 12 p_boundary). The second half is graded from frame 110,808
   but kicks off at 113,742 (DATA.md): those frames are written D.

## 4. Training data

Every label used to train the submission comes with a public dataset of **other matches**: it is
the provider's own, or it is computed by our code from the provider's events. None of them was
made by hand by us, and **no label of the provided game is used**.

| dataset (source) | amount | who labelled, and how | what it trains |
|---|---|---|---|
| DFL / Sportec Solutions open data, 7 Bundesliga matches (Bassek et al. 2025, CC BY 4.0; Hugging Face `pysport/idsse-data`) | 668 labelled minutes, 1.00 M frames at 25 Hz: home 29%, away 26%, dead ball 45%; 2,351 changes of team | the provider: the possession flag and the ball-status flag carried by every frame of the published tracking, part of DFL's official match data. Used as published | every model but the boundary detector: the 9 boosting team models, the 16 networks, the 3 dead-ball models, the change detector |
| PFF FC 2022 FIFA World Cup, 64 matches (free after sign-up) | 108 hours, 9.73 M frames: home 31%, away 30%, dead ball 40% | the provider's event data (PFF FC), turned into per-frame labels by our code (`possession/pff.py`): the team of the last controlled on-ball action; dead ball from a ball out, or the end of the last action before a set piece, until the restart | the 3 dead-ball models (with DFL), the dead-ball boundary detector |
| Metrica Sports sample data, 3 anonymised matches (public) | 286 labelled minutes, 0.43 M frames; 846 possessions | the provider's event data (Metrica Sports), turned into per-frame labels by our code (`possession/metrica_open.py`), then the challenge's controlled-possession rule (section 6) | 8 of the 16 networks; also the validation set (section 6) |

- **How much of it each model sees.** Boosting models: every 2nd frame of two noisy copies of
  each DFL match; the dead-ball models add one frame in 4 of each PFF match. Networks: 76,800
  windows of 20 s drawn at random from the noisy copies (twice that for one variant), a Metrica
  window three times as often as its length says. Change detector: the frames within 0.24 s of
  DFL's 2,351 changes of team against calm live frames; boundary detector: the same around PFF's
  stoppages and restarts.
- **Metrica's matches both train and validate.** A network scored on a Metrica match was trained
  without that match (leave one match out); the 8 networks in the submission are trained on all
  three. The boosting models and the detectors never see them.
- **What the provided game contributes to training, without labels:** where its camera stands
  (from the provided homographies: airborne balls are drawn along its line of sight in the
  training copies) and where its ball is missing. The training copies lose the ball at the same
  moments (a stretch of our match's gaps laid over each copy), or at the same rate per game
  state, the state coming from a first model's decoding of our match. No person decided any of
  it. The 5 public minutes are never trained on.
- **Labelled data that only validates:** SkillCorner open data (MIT), 20 A-League matches, 4,867
  possessions computed by our code from SkillCorner's player possessions and phases of play
  (`possession/skillcorner.py`); never trained on.
- **Labelled data in experiments only**, in no model of the submission: SoccerTrack v2 (ball
  actions with team, by its authors) and SoccerNet Game State Reconstruction 2025 (boxes, teams
  and pitch positions, by its authors).
- **Pre-trained models:** none in the submission.

## 5. Possession rules

We do not hand-code them. The team convention is learned from DFL's flags (a team gains the
ball at its touch) and the dead ball from DFL's referee status plus PFF's events (ball out until
the restart). The decoder then imposes possessions as intervals.

DFL's flag changes at every touch, far more often than a possession convention does. The
decoder's switching cost keeps only changes with sustained evidence. Its costs were set on
Metrica's matches:
- The submission has 220 possessions over the match, **4.1 per live minute**, close to the
  official public window's 4.2.
- The previous, higher costs gave 3.6 per live minute and under-predicted possessions on the
  Metrica set.

Training on labels closer to "controlled possession" (DFL's events, or with short opponent
spells removed) gives cleaner frames but worse possessions on the Metrica validation set.

## 6. Validation (and how we avoided overfitting)

| sample | possessions | role |
|---|---|---|
| **Metrica validation set** (`scripts/metrica_val.py`): Metrica's 3 open matches with our noise, scored against possession built from Metrica's own events (`possession/metrica_open.py`) | 1,132 | decides every choice; never trained on |
| SkillCorner set (`scripts/skillcorner_val.py`): 20 open A-League matches, broadcast tracking as provided, scored against possession built from SkillCorner's own events by the same control rule | 4,904 | a check on real tracking noise; never trained on |
| official public window | 17 | the only real answers for this match; a check, too small to rank versions |
| full match, no labels (`tools/sanity_full_match.py`) | n/a | possession rate, dead-ball runs, per-block statistics |
| DFL matches (`scripts/model_vs_information.py`) | thousands | what the tracking's noise costs |

- **Paired bootstraps over possessions** (`scripts/validate_clean.py`, `tools/compare.py`): both
  versions are scored on the same resampled possessions.
- **Validation labels in the challenge's convention.** CHALLENGE.md defines a change of
  possession as another team gaining *controlled* possession (a touch, a deflection or an aerial
  duel won without keeping the ball does not count; a loose ball stays with the last team). Our
  Metrica labels gave the ball away at every recovery: 26% of possessions under 2 s, 6.4 per live
  minute, against 12% and 4.2 in the official window. With the rule (a spell won in open play
  counts if its team passes, shoots or keeps the ball 2 s; `possession/metrica_open.CONTROL_RULES`)
  the Metrica set has the window's structure (< 5 s 37% vs 35%, median 8.0 vs 7.6 s, 4.8 per live
  minute) and **agrees with the official window**: everything that split possessions more had
  looked good on the old labels and bad on the window; on the new ones it is bad on both.
- **Validation labels checked against physics.** Our first conversion of Metrica's events ended a
  stoppage only at a "set piece" event, but Metrica often records goal kicks as a recovery and a
  pass, and ~1 restart in 5 follows an event that is not a stoppage (offside, ball lost out of
  play). 10-17% of frames were wrong: "live" frames with no ball and players at walking pace.
  Corrected (every restart is preceded by dead ball, which lasts until the next play of the ball;
  live frames without a ball fell from 8-18% to 2-4.5%), and every choice re-checked on it.
- **Decoder costs:** a grid of about 170 settings, then re-checked on the corrected labels. Team
  cost 28 with bonus 23 sits on a broad plateau (team cost 20-32, bonus 0.8-1x the team cost);
  the corrected labels moved the dead-ball cost down to 24.
- **Training variance:** one model's score moves by ~0.02-0.03 with its noise draw, so we submit
  averages over draws.
- **Model or information?** (`scripts/model_vs_information.py`): on the DFL matches, the same
  pipeline reaches spellF1 ~0.70 with perfect tracking and ~0.58 with our noise, while ~90% of
  frames get the right team. Our tracking's noise costs ~0.12, and turning frames into
  possessions costs more. The change detector learned on DFL carries over and is in the
  submission; so are the temporal networks (they looked useless on our first, wrong Metrica
  labels). The boundary net helps on DFL but not on the Metrica set.

| version | official window (17) | Metrica validation (1,132) |
|---|---|---|
| official baseline_smooth50 (full match 0.243) | 0.326 | |
| our first model (in-domain, tracking + Viterbi) | 0.571 | |
| DFL models, previous decoder (48 / 24 / 128) | 0.622 (macroF1 0.861) | 0.602 (macroF1 0.908) |
| DFL models, decoder 28 / 23 / 64 | 0.627 (macroF1 0.865) | 0.647 (macroF1 0.913) |
| + dead ball from DFL + PFF, boundary detector, 28 / 23 / 24 | 0.653 (macroF1 0.930) | 0.654 (macroF1 0.916) |
| + team from boosting with physical signals and 8 temporal networks | 0.690 (macroF1 0.933) | 0.677 (macroF1 0.922) |
| **submission** (+ 8 networks trained with Metrica's matches) | **0.690** (macroF1 0.934) | **0.794** in the challenge's convention (0.810 with the strict rule) |

Metrica scores on the dead-ball-corrected labels; the last line on the controlled-possession
labels, the convention the official window agrees with.

**Full-match estimate:** the official baseline scores 0.75x on the full match what it scores on
the public window (0.243 vs 0.326). The same ratio puts our submission near **spellF1 0.51**.
The Metrica set in the challenge's convention, a different domain, gives 0.79.

## 7. Limitations

- **Short possessions** (1-4 s: interceptions, clearances, lost balls) are mostly missed. The
  tracking (~1 m precision) does not show who touched the ball in a duel. The image helps only
  where the contact is visible, which is rare at those moments. Even exact player positions get
  only 72% of duels right (SoccerTrack).
- **Domain gap:** the DFL matches are far from ours for a classifier (AUC 0.995: league, style,
  noise). Matching the simulation's low-level statistics did not change what the models learn.
- **Only 17 possessions of this match have answers.** The decoder is tuned on a proxy: Metrica's
  open matches, from another league and season, with possession derived from events by our own
  rules. **The team estimate is the bottleneck** (`scripts/metrica_anatomy.py`): on that proxy,
  perfect team probabilities would give 0.95 against 0.65 (before the temporal networks, which
  brought it to 0.68); perfect dead ball 0.68, a perfect change detector 0.67.
- **Dead-ball share:** the submission marks 48% of the graded frames as dead ball, against 20% in
  the public window. The longest runs are stoppages where players stand still and the ball is
  unseen. How much of the rest is real is unknown.

**With more time / data:** the Innovation Challenge's 10 Metrica matches would allow training
directly on the provider's convention, which no open dataset offers at scale. A touch detector
with team would need far more annotated video than the open sets provide.

## 8. Reproduction

```bash
python -m venv .venv && .venv/bin/pip install -r requirements.txt
tools/download_dfl.sh                         # 2.6 GB, CC-BY 4.0 (Hugging Face pysport/idsse-data)
tools/download_pff.sh <drive-folder-id>       # 3.7 GB, free after sign-up (see the script)
tools/download_metrica.sh                     # 175 MB, Metrica's open sample data
# match data unpacked into repo/data/ (not in this repository)
.venv/bin/python scripts/make_submission.py   # -> outputs/submissions/dfl_bag_st_dflcd.csv
```

Deterministic (fixed seeds throughout; a network or a boosting model retrained from scratch gives
identical probabilities). CPU only (about four hours on a laptop, mostly building the noisy DFL
copies, reading the PFF tracking and training the networks). Metrica's sample data trains 8 of
the networks and is the validation set.
